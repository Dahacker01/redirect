Ticket Workshop

Write a ticket someone can actually use.

Pick a situation from your own life, write the ticket, and use the live checklist to see whether another person could act on it without having to guess.

Good tickets usually answer:
  • What is happening?
  • What did you expect?
  • How can someone reproduce it?
  • What does “done” look like?
Practice challenge: Choose a real, non-sensitive problem from your life — a broken device, a class/work task, a household problem, an app issue, or something similar.
Don't enter passwords, private keys, financial information, or other sensitive personal data.

Why these fields?

Title

Make it searchable and specific. Avoid titles like “It doesn't work.”

Problem + expected result

Separating actual behavior from expected behavior makes the gap clear.

Reproduction steps

Another person should have enough information to try the same sequence.

Environment

The same problem may only occur on a particular device, version, network, or situation.

Acceptance criteria

Define what a successful fix or completed task should accomplish.

Quick exercise

After writing your ticket, swap with someone else.

  1. Have them read only the ticket.
  2. Ask what they think the problem is.
  3. Ask what they would do next.
  4. Compare their answer with what you intended.

If they have to ask several basic questions, improve the ticket.
